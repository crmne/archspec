ArchSpec 1.2.1 fixes false reports from `cannot_reference_includers` that 1.2.0 made visible in apps with namespaced concerns, and makes `cannot_call` catch calls on a gem's own constants. The second fix can turn a passing check red, in the places where a rule was quietly not working.

## Fixed

- **A concern reading its own constants no longer counts as naming its includer.** `Chat::Interruptions::ClassMethods` reading `Chat::Interruptions::GRACE` was reported as referencing `Chat`. By @crmne ([#61](https://github.com/crmne/archspec/issues/61), [#62](https://github.com/crmne/archspec/pull/62)).
- **A class nested in the includer's namespace is its own constant.** `Card::Closure` read from `Card::Closeable` passes now, so a shared concern reaching into `Order::Line` passes too. `Order` and its value constants, such as `Order::LIMIT`, still fail. By @crmne ([#62](https://github.com/crmne/archspec/pull/62)).
- **`Worker.prepend(Poller)` makes `Worker` the includer, not the class whose body makes the call.** `Worker` also gets `Poller`'s methods, even when `Poller` is named from another class's scope. By @crmne ([#62](https://github.com/crmne/archspec/pull/62)).
- **`cannot_call` matches a receiver a gem declares.** `receiver: "ActiveRecord::Base"` caught `User.find_by_sql` but not `ActiveRecord::Base.find_by_sql`, and a rule such as `cannot_call :mcp, receiver: "RubyLLM"` never fired. Expect new violations if you have rules like these. By @crmne ([#62](https://github.com/crmne/archspec/pull/62)).

## Known limitations

- **`method_names.requires` looks for the sibling across the whole component,** as documented, not on the same class. `subscribe_later` on one model is satisfied by `subscribe_now` on another.
- **Rails reflection records a gap for associations a macro declares,** such as `acts_as_tenant :account`, because it has no literal declaration to point at.
